Welcome back to the podcast. Today we are going to talk about product-market fit,
and specifically the difference between a value hypothesis and a growth hypothesis.
This is a framework I wish I had internalized years earlier because it would have
saved my team months of building the wrong thing.

The core idea is simple but powerful. Before you can grow anything, you need to
know that a meaningful number of people get real value from your product. That is
the value hypothesis. It asks a single question: do users find the product valuable
enough to use it repeatedly? The growth hypothesis is a separate question: once you
have value, how do you reach more people who will also find value?

Most teams make the mistake of testing the growth hypothesis before the value
hypothesis is true. They buy ads, spin up referral programs, and optimize the top
of the funnel while the core product still has no retention. That is like pouring
water into a bucket with a hole in the bottom. You can pour faster and faster, but
you are not actually filling anything.

A better sequence is to define a target user, identify the single job they are
hiring your product to do, and then measure whether they come back on their own.
If your early users are not returning without a nudge, you do not have product-market
fit yet, and no amount of marketing will fix that.

One practical way to measure the value hypothesis is a simple cohort analysis.
Look at the people who signed up in a given week. What percentage of them are still
active four weeks later? If that number is flat or declining, you have a leaky
bucket. If it is stable and even ticking up, you have the beginnings of something.

Another signal I like is what I call the toothbrush test. Does your product get used
at least once or twice a week, the way people brush their teeth? If someone can go
a month without opening your app and not feel any pain, you probably have a nice-to-have
rather than a must-have.

It is also important to separate retention from engagement. Retention asks whether
people come back. Engagement asks how deeply they use the product when they are
there. You can have high engagement among a tiny, loyal group and still be far from
product-market fit because the group never grows. The goal is retention at scale.

Finally, remember that product-market fit is a milestone, not a permanent state.
Markets change, competitors ship, and your users' needs evolve. The teams that keep
their value hypothesis alive are the ones that keep talking to customers every week
and re-testing their assumptions.

So the takeaway today is this: validate value before you chase growth. Define your
target user, find the job to be done, and measure whether they return on their own.
If they do not, fix the product first. Everything else is premature.
