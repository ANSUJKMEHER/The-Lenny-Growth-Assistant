Today we are talking about positioning, which might be the most underrated skill in
product and growth. You can have the best product in the world, but if you cannot
explain what it is and who it is for in one sentence, people will never find out.

Let me start with a definition. Positioning is not your tagline and it is not your
brand. Positioning is the act of choosing the market category you compete in and the
specific customer you serve, and then making your product the obvious choice for that
customer in that category. It is a strategic decision you make, not a slogan you write.

The classic mistake is trying to be for everyone. When you say your product is for
everyone, it is for no one. The customer cannot tell if it is for them, and your
messaging becomes so generic that it fails to connect with anyone. The sharper your
target customer, the easier everything downstream becomes: your copy, your ads, your
onboarding, even your product roadmap.

A useful exercise is to write a positioning statement. It does not need to be public.
It just needs to force clarity. A simple template is: for [target customer] who
[has this problem], [product name] is a [category] that [delivers this benefit],
unlike [the alternative], because [the differentiator]. If you cannot fill that in
without hesitating, you have work to do.

Once positioning is clear, messaging becomes the job of translating it into language
your customer already uses. Do not describe your product in the words your engineers
use. Use the words your customers use when they describe their problem. The best way
to find those words is to read support tickets, sales call notes, and app store
reviews, and literally borrow their phrases.

There is also a hierarchy to messaging that I find useful. Start with the value
proposition at the top, then the benefits, then the features. Too many companies lead
with features, which is backwards. Customers do not buy features. They buy outcomes.
Lead with the outcome, support it with the feature.

A concrete example: instead of saying "we have AI-powered scheduling", say "never
waste an hour going back and forth over email again". The second one speaks to a
pain the customer feels. The first one speaks to a feature you built. Same product,
completely different impact.

Finally, remember that positioning is not set and forget. As your product matures and
your market shifts, you may need to reposition. The telltale sign is when your
messaging no longer matches what your best customers actually love about the product.
When that happens, go back to your customers, listen, and rewrite the statement.

The takeaway: pick a specific customer, pick a category, and lead with the outcome.
Do that, and every other growth lever you pull will work a little bit better.
